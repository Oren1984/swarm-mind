# Narration script (English)

This is the narration exactly as rendered in `showcase/video/swarm-mind-demo.mp4`. It is generated from
[`scripts/video_script.json`](../../scripts/video_script.json); the numbers are inserted at build time from
`experiments/results/summary.json`, so the script cannot drift from the stored results. Each sentence below is
one narration utterance and one subtitle cue.

## Scene 1 — Introduction (0:00.0–0:08.3)

*On screen: Title card over a blurred real screenshot of the dashboard.*

- Swarm Mind. Intelligence without a leader.
- Can simple agents solve a problem together, when nobody is in charge?

## Scene 2 — The problem (0:08.3–0:21.6)

*On screen: Screen recording: World A (Independent), from step 0.*

- In this simulated world, 60 agents leave the nest to search for food.
- Each one explores on its own, picks up food, and carries it home.
- No agent shares what it finds. This is our baseline.

## Scene 3 — Local communication (0:21.6–0:38.1)

*On screen: Screen recording: World B (Collective), from step 0; trails form.*

- Now we change one thing. Agents carrying food leave a pheromone mark behind them.
- Other agents can smell these marks nearby, and tend to follow them.
- Nobody draws these trails. They emerge from many small local choices, and fade when they are no longer used.

## Scene 4 — Three-world comparison (0:38.1–0:50.5)

*On screen: Screen recording: all three worlds with the live chart and table.*

- Three worlds run side by side: the same map, the same agents, the same random seed.
- The only difference is communication. None, pheromone, and pheromone that will be cut off halfway.

## Scene 5 — Communication breakdown (0:50.5–1:05.8)

*On screen: Screen recording: World C; the scheduled cut at step 1,500 happens on screen.*

- In the third world, at step 1,500, we switch communication off.
- The old trails stay visible for a while, but no agent can smell them, and they fade away.
- The swarm does not stop. It falls back to searching alone.

## Scene 6 — Measured results (1:05.8–1:39.2)

*On screen: Three experiment charts generated by the experiment runner.*

- Across 20 random seeds, the communicating swarm delivered 1,279 units of food on average, against 561 for independent agents.
- After the cut, the third world delivered 302 units, close to the independent baseline of 275.
- The uninterrupted swarm delivered 654 in the same period.
- The advantage is not universal. With fast evaporation, it shrinks to 13 percent.
- And when trails never fade while food keeps moving, the communicating swarm did 4 percent worse.

## Scene 7 — Research and transparency (1:39.2–1:59.0)

*On screen: Closing card over a blurred real screenshot.*

- This is a research-inspired proof of concept, built on ideas from Deneubourg, Dorigo, and Reynolds.
- All data is synthetic. No language model is involved. The results describe this simulation only, not real ants.
- Swarm Mind. Simple rules, local signals, collective behaviour.
